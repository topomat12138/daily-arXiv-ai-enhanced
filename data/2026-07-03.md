### [1] [Fabrication of high-quality topological insulator nanodevices from bulk-insulating air-sensitive Sb-Bi$_2$Se$_3$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01439)
*Linh T. Dang, Ayushi Solanki, Yongjian Wang, Oliver Breunig, Yoichi Ando*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01439)

**TL;DR**: Sb-substituted Bi2Se3 (SBS) can retain very low bulk carrier density through a room-temperature microfabrication protocol; devices (Hall bars and nanowires) show quantum-interference oscillations, strong gate tunability and clear weak antilocalization, indicating SBS is a promising non‑compensated TI platform for TI–superconductor Majorana devices.  
**Method**: Systematic study of the effect of thermal processing during microfabrication; development and implementation of a room‑temperature fabrication protocol; low‑temperature transport measurements on exfoliated SBS Hall bars and nanowires, identifying quantum interference oscillations, gate dependence and weak antilocalization as diagnostics of preserved low carrier density and coherence.  
**Tags**: `topology, quantum_devices, quantum_computing, topological_insulator, microfabrication, quantum_transport, weak_antilocalization, Majorana`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
High-quality topological insulator (TI) materials are essential for the realization and detection of Majorana bound states (MBSs) in TI-superconductor hybrid platforms. Widely used compensated TIs exhibit substantial disorder and charge inhomogeneity, which may be detrimental for Majorana devices. In this regard, Sb-substituted Bi$_2$Se$_3$ (SBS) is promising, because it is non-compensated and yet achieves very low bulk carrier density. We systematically investigate the impact of thermal processing during microfabrication on the transport properties of SBS. We developed a room-temperature fabrication protocol that preserves the low carrier density of exfoliated SBS upon fabrication of Hall bar and nanowire devices as evidenced from the observation of quantum interference oscillations in nanowires, a large gate tunability, and clear signatures of weak antilocalization (WAL).
</details>


### [2] [The Wigner function for Integer quantum Hall effect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02094)
*Yao Wang, Qi-Ming Fu, Huawei Fan, Li Yu, Kang Li*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02094)

**TL;DR**: Compute and present the Wigner quasi-probability distribution for integer quantum Hall single-particle states: authors review Landau‑gauge electronic wavefunctions, summarize Wigner‑function properties, and evaluate the phase‑space Wigner function for the integer quantum Hall effect.  
**Method**: Start from single‑particle Landau‑gauge wavefunctions in an electromagnetic field; review Wigner‑function properties; compute the Wigner distribution for integer quantum Hall states by performing the defining phase‑space integral (integral method) on the Landau‑level wavefunctions.  
**Tags**: `topology, quantum_geometry, Wigner_function, integer_quantum_Hall, Landau_gauge, phase_space`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Wigner's quasi-probability distribution function in phase space is a specialized representation of the density matrix, possessing significant physical importance. In this article, we first review the wave function describing electronic motion in an electromagnetic field under the Landau gauge. Next, based on an introduction to the properties of the Wigner function, we calculate the Wigner function for the integer quantum Hall effect using the integral method.
</details>


### [3] [Intrinsic orbital Hall effect in a nonuniform electric field](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02418)
*Min Ju Park, Jongjun M. Lee, Hyun-Woo Lee*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02418)

**TL;DR**: Analytic derivation linking intrinsic orbital Hall conductivity (dc, spatially nonuniform E-field) to Bloch‑state quantum geometry: the response is expressed in terms of orbital Berry curvature and quantum metric, mirroring the geometric formulation of intrinsic charge Hall transport. A tight‑binding example shows higher‑order orbital Hall terms can be strongly orientation‑dependent in anisotropic samples.  
**Method**: Analytic geometric formulation of orbital angular momentum transport using Bloch wavefunction quantities (orbital Berry curvature and quantum metric) for the intrinsic orbital Hall effect in the dc limit with a spatially nonuniform electric field, supplemented by a tight‑binding model calculation to illustrate higher‑order/orientation sensitivity.  
**Tags**: `quantum_geometry, orbital_Hall, Berry_curvature, quantum_metric, charge_Hall, tight_binding, anisotropy, Bloch_states`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Geometric analysis of electronic Bloch states offers a universal framework for understanding electronic properties, yet its role in the transport of orbital angular momentum remains unexplored. In this work, we establish an analytic connection between orbital angular momentum transport and the geometric properties of Bloch wave functions in electronic systems. Focusing on the intrinsic orbital Hall effect in the dc limit under a spatially nonuniform electric field, we show that its conductivity can be expressed in terms of universal geometric quantities, such as the orbital Berry curvature and quantum metric. This formulation provides a term-by-term correspondence with the geometric description of intrinsic charge Hall transport established in previous studies. Using a tight-binding model, we further illustrate that the higher-order orbital Hall response can exhibit enhanced sensitivity to the orientation of an anisotropic sample. Our work deepens the understanding of diverse intrinsic transverse transport phenomena and the role of quantum geometry in electronic systems.
</details>
