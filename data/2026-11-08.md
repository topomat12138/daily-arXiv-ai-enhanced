### [1] [Probing Non-Abelian Topology through Quench Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07456)
*Zhenming Zhang, Tianyu Li, Wei Yi*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07456)

**TL;DR**: Proposes a quench protocol and a band-projection analysis that uses dynamic skyrmions in quasimomentum–time manifolds to extract gap-resolved dynamic Chern numbers and thereby reconstruct the full non‑Abelian topology (global non‑Abelian charge and edge‑state patterns) of multiband post‑quench Hamiltonians; implementable in photonic or cold‑atom platforms.  
**Method**: Quench the system into a target multiband Hamiltonian, project dynamics onto individual band gaps (band‑projection scheme), analyze emergent quasimomentum–time manifolds to identify dynamic skyrmions and compute gap‑resolved dynamic Chern numbers, then reconstruct the full non‑Abelian topological invariants and predict edge‑state patterns under open boundaries.  
**Tags**: `quench dynamics, non‑Abelian topology, multiband systems, topological invariants, quantum simulation`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Quench dynamics offer valuable insights to topological matter, wherein the emergent topological phenomena serve as a dynamic probe to the topology of static Hamiltonians. While such a practice has been widely applied to detect the Abelian band topology or the Euler-class topology of band nodes, little is known regarding the quench dynamics of non-Abelian topological insulators in multiband systems, where the intertwining geometry of all eigenstates necessitates the description of noncommutative topological charges. Here we propose a quench protocol for non-Abelian topological insulators, so that the underlying topology can be systematically probed using dynamic signatures. Specifically, we develop a band-projection scheme to extract the gap-resolved topology from dynamic skyrmions in the emergent quasimomentum--time manifolds of the quench processes. These skyrmions are characterized by a series of gap-resolved dynamic Chern numbers, which enable us to reconstruct the full non-Abelian topology of the post-quench Hamiltonian, including the global non-Abelian topological charge and edge-state patterns under the open-boundary condition. Our proposal offers dynamical access to non-Abelian topology in multiband systems, and can be readily implemented using photons or cold atoms.

**Tags**: `non-Abelian braiding, topological pumping, edge pumping, multi-gap topology, integrated photonics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Non-Abelian operations enable sequence-dependent control of light, yet experimental realizations in wave systems have remained confined to local bulk or same-boundary modes and have often required fragile gap-closing degeneracies. Conventional Thouless pumps, meanwhile, support only two-channel edge transfer across a single gap. Here we demonstrate multi-channel non-Abelian edge pumping on a silicon photonic chip by adiabatically encircling multiple band degeneracies in a multi-gap lattice. In a minimal three-band band-triangle, two opposite-charge Weyl points and one zero-charge degeneracy are linked by global band connectivity, yielding both opposite-boundary and same-boundary edge channels. <details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
The nonlinear interaction of closely spaced magnetic skyrmions remains poorly understood, particularly the conditions governing their merger into a single skyrmion or a stable skyrmionium. Here, we investigate the merging dynamics of two Néel skyrmions using analytical modelling and micromagnetic simulations based on Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert dynamics. We examine the effects of exchange, Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, anisotropy, and magnetostatic energies by varying the initial separation, polarity, winding number, and skyrmion size. These findings establish a predictive framework for controlling skyrmion merging and provide design guidelines for engineering skyrmionium textures in chiral magnetic nanostructures.

### [4] [Topological Transitions of Evanescent Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07038)

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07038)

**TL;DR**: The authors analyze evanescent (decaying/growing) gap modes of Hermitian tight-binding systems, showing these modes have non-Hermitian dispersions with two kinds of exceptional points: standard band-edge EPs and a distinct pairwise in-gap EP protected by spinless time-reversal symmetry. **Tags**: `non-Hermitian physics, topological band theory, impurity physics, photonic crystals, tight-binding models`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We study the band structure of evanescent waves, that is, the decaying or growing solutions that live within the gaps of tight-binding Hamiltonians. Although the underlying Hamiltonian is Hermitian, these modes carry complex wavevectors and therefore exhibit a non-Hermitian dispersion. Using the transfer matrix formalism in one dimension, we show that, in addition to the standard exceptional points that appear at the band edges, a different type of exceptional point can arise inside the gap, protected by spinless time-reversal symmetry. Tuning model parameters drives a topological transition where such in-gap exceptional points are nucleated in pairs, mediated by a crossing between purely imaginary momentum branches. We show that this spectral structure is diagnosed by a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ index assigned to every energy inside the gap, with the in-gap exceptional points arising as its domain walls. A generic bound state in the system has a tail that is purely exponential when its energy lies between a band edge and the nearest exceptional point, but becomes oscillatory - with a wavevector differing from the band-edge momentum - when its energy lies between the pair of in-gap exceptional points. We illustrate these results using continuum and tight-binding models, as well as an anisotropic photonic crystal.
</details>

### [15] [Universal Negative Correlation between Nonreciprocity and Superconducting Transition Width in FeTe-Based Heterostructures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07116)
*Annie G. Wang, Saswata Mandal, Zi-Jie Yan, Bomin Zhang, Xiaoda Liu, Hongtao Rong, Deyi Zhuo, Pu Xiao, Lok-Kan Lai, Zhiyuan Xi, Ke Wang, Chao-Xing Liu, Cui-Zu Chang*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07116)

**TL;DR**: MBE-grown FeTe-based heterostructures with widely varying top layers show that nonreciprocal superconducting transport (magnetochiral anisotropy γ) is insensitive to top-layer crystal structure, thickness, topology, or magnetism, but scales negatively with superconducting transition width ΔT. Control FeTe films show weak nonreciprocity, implicating interfacial inversion-symmetry breaking. A disorder-based theoretical model and measurements indicate that, for comparable inversion-symmetry breaking, reduced interfacial disorder (smaller ΔT) sharpens the transition and strongly enhances nonreciprocal response in the BKT regime, making ΔT a unifying tuning parameter for the superconducting diode effect.  
**Method**: Molecular beam epitaxy synthesis of a family of FeTe-based heterostructures with varied top layers, systematic first- and second-harmonic transport measurements (to extract magnetochiral anisotropy γ), control measurements on stoichiometric FeTe films, and comparison with a disorder-based theoretical model focused on the BKT transition regime.  
**Tags**: `superconducting diode effect, nonreciprocal transport, interfacial effects, impurity physics, BKT transition, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Nonreciprocal charge transport in noncentrosymmetric superconductors probes broken symmetries and superconducting fluctuations and underpins the superconducting diode effect. Despite extensive studies, the factors governing its magnitude remain unclear. In this work, we use molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) to synthesize a broad family of FeTe-based superconducting heterostructures with top layers spanning topologically trivial versus nontrivial, layered versus nonlayered, and magnetic versus nonmagnetic materials. Through systematic first- and second-harmonic transport measurements, we find that the magnetochiral anisotropy coefficient $γ$ is insensitive to the top-layer crystal structure, lattice constant, thickness, topology, and magnetism, but exhibits a negative correlation with the superconducting transition width $ΔT$. Control measurements on superconducting stoichiometric FeTe films reveal much weaker nonreciprocity, confirming the essential role of interfacial inversion-symmetry breaking. Combined with a disorder-based theoretical model, our results establish a universal relationship: for comparable inversion-symmetry breaking, reduced interfacial disorder sharpens the superconducting transition and strongly enhances nonreciprocal transport in the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition regime. These findings identify $ΔT$ as a unifying parameter governing nonreciprocity across FeTe-based heterostructures, reconcile previously disparate observations, and establish a general framework for engineering enhanced nonreciprocal superconducting responses and exploring superconducting diode effects in nonlinear quantum devices.

### [16] [Majorana corner modes and tunable braiding in an altermagnet-superconductor heterostructure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07645)
*Yingli Gao, Kai Zhang, Ling-Zheng Meng, Junpeng Hou, Ning Li, Ya-Jie Wu*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07645)

**TL;DR**: Proposes a zero-net-field route to Majorana corner modes (MCMs) using an altermagnet–superconductor heterostructure on a honeycomb lattice: altermagnetism produces inequivalent effective edge masses for bearded and zigzag terminations, enabling corner-localized Majorana modes. The paper also shows how to implement a complete set of Clifford gates by tuning altermagnetic anisotropy and chemical potential.  
**Method**: Analytical low-energy edge-theory applied to a honeycomb-lattice altermagnet–superconductor heterostructure; identification of edge-dependent effective mass terms and derivation of conditions for MCM emergence for bearded and zigzag edges; explicit construction of Majorana Clifford gates via parameter tuning (altermagnetic anisotropy and chemical potential).  
**Tags**: `topological superconductivity, Majorana physics, altermagnetism, superconducting heterostructures, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Zeeman fields are usually required for realizing and manipulating Majorana corner modes at the cost of suppressed superconducting pairing. Altermagnetism offers a new route toward realizing Majorana corner modes under zero net external magnetic fields. Here we propose a theoretical scheme to host Majorana corner modes in a honeycomb-lattice altermagnet-superconductor heterostructure. In contrast to previous studies on square lattices, altermagnetism yields distinct effective low-energy edge masses on various inequivalent edges of the honeycomb lattice. Within a low-energy edge-theory framework, we establish the conditions under which Majorana corner modes emerge for both bearded and zigzag edge terminations. We further explicitly construct a complete set of Clifford gates by tuning the altermagnetic anisotropy and chemical potential of the heterostructure. Our results provide a promising platform for the realization and manipulation of Majorana corner modes.

### [17] [Microscopic Origin and Josephson Signatures of $d$-Wave Superconductivity in Monolayer FeSe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08037)

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08037)

**Method**: Constructed an experimentally‑constrained multiorbital tight‑binding model (fit to QPI and ARPES), then analyzed pairing with itinerant spin‑fluctuation and strong‑coupling exchange calculations; performed symmetry analysis of spatial Josephson tunneling and modelled impurity and vortex effects.  
**Tags**: `unconventional superconductivity, multiorbital superconductivity, Josephson physics, impurity physics, vortex physics, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Monolayer FeSe on SrTiO$_3$ exhibits high-temperature superconductivity with an electron-only Fermi surface, raising fundamental questions about its unconventional pairing mechanism. Motivated by recent Josephson scanning tunneling microscopy evidence for nodeless $d$-wave superconductivity, we investigate the microscopic role of the Fe $d_{xy}$ orbital using an experimentally constrained multiorbital theory. A tight-binding model fitted to quasiparticle interference and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy reveals substantial $d_{xy}$ character near the Fermi level and dominant nearest-neighbor hopping, in contrast to the conventional kinetic-frustration picture of iron-based superconductors. Both itinerant spin-fluctuation calculations and strong-coupling exchange approaches favor a robust $d$-wave pairing state, whose interband pairing components allow a fully gapped quasiparticle spectrum. We further establish a symmetry-based selection rule for spatial Josephson tunneling, revealing characteristic bond-centered maxima and mirror-enforced interference zeros that distinguish $d$-wave pairing from competing $s$-wave states. These symmetry-enforced nodal lines survive near Fe-site impurities, while a pronounced contrast between the $d$- and $s$-wave Josephson patterns persists around vortices despite the loss of mirror protection. Our results connect the unconventional $d_{xy}$ electronic structure to nodeless $d$-wave superconductivity and demonstrate the power of spatial Josephson interference for resolving pairing symmetry in multiorbital superconductors.
</details>

**Tags**: `charge-density waves, dimensionality in superconductivity, quasi-one-dimensional systems, electrostatic gating / tunable superconductivity, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Dimensionality governs the stability of collective quantum states, including whether Cooper pairs can establish the phase coherence required for superconductivity. In solids, however, electronic dimensionality is usually encoded in the crystal structure and is difficult to control reversibly. Here we show evidence that monolayer 1$T^{\prime}$-MoTe$_2$ develops a stripe charge-density wave that reorganizes its low-energy electronic states into quasi-one-dimensional channels. Raman spectroscopy and scanning tunneling microscopy identify the stripe order, whereas directional transport and tunneling spectroscopy reveal superconducting correlations confined to the stripe direction and insulating behavior perpendicular to it. Electrostatic doping weakens the transverse insulating response and drives the system into phase-coherent two-dimensional superconductivity. These results show that stripe charge order can reconfigure superconducting dimensionality and make it electrically tunable within a single crystalline layer.

### [19] [Fermi-volume anomaly in quartet condensates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07601)
*Yixin Guo*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07601)

**TL;DR**: Study of Fermi-volume counting in a four-component Fermi system with quartet (four-fermion) order. The authors express the correction to Luttinger-volume count as the response of the Luttinger–Ward functional to a phase winding of the quartet source; the leading contribution is a weighted difference of four-particle and four-hole states. For a Gaussian interaction vertex and parabolic band, the correction expands the Fermi volume in 1D, contracts it in 3D (within the parameter range studied), and has a cutoff-dependent sign in 2D. They solve the 1D Dyson equation at fixed density and find the counting correction sensitive to vertex width (while fermionic entropy is nearly unchanged); they also analyze a linear pairing response that can prevent continuation of the unpaired branch to T→0.  
**Method**: Field-theoretic analysis using the Luttinger–Ward functional; compute Fermi-volume correction via its response to a quartet-source phase winding. Analytic expansion for a Gaussian vertex and parabolic dispersion in 1–3D, plus numerical solution of the one-dimensional Dyson equation at fixed density and low temperature; comparison of Fermi-point counting vs dressed-spectrum evaluation and study of linear pairing susceptibility.  
**Tags**: `unconventional superconductivity, Luttinger theorem, quantum many-body theory, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the relation between density and Fermi volume in a four-component Fermi system with quartet order. For a regular zero-temperature branch without pair order, we express the correction to the Fermi-volume count in terms of the response of the Luttinger-Ward functional to a phase winding of the quartet source. Its leading term is a weighted difference between four-particle and four-hole states. For a Gaussian vertex and parabolic dispersion, we find that the correction expands the Fermi volume in one dimension and contracts it in three dimensions in the parameter range studied. The sign in two dimensions depends on the momentum cutoff. We solve the one-dimensional Dyson equation at fixed density and compare the counting correction obtained from the Fermi points with that evaluated from the dressed spectra at low temperature. At fixed quartet amplitude and peak normalization of the vertex, the correction depends strongly on the vertex width, whereas the fermionic entropy changes little. We also examine the linear pairing response, which can limit the continuation of the unpaired branch to zero temperature.
</details>

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07708)

**TL;DR**: Twisted cuprate Josephson junctions exhibit an anomalous Shapiro-step sequence nhf/3e (unit hf/3e), interpreted as a Josephson tunneling process that transfers trios of electrons (3e) rather than conventional Cooper pairs. Frequency/power, temperature and current-annealing dependences support hf/3e as the effective charge unit and point to an interplay with competing charge orders in the twisted bilayer system.  
**Method**: Experimental microwave-irradiated Josephson transport measurements on twisted cuprate Josephson junctions, analyzing Shapiro step sequences as functions of ac frequency/power, temperature, and current annealing to identify the effective charge quantum.  
**Tags**: `unconventional superconductivity, Josephson physics, twisted bilayer systems (twistronics), charge ordering / competing orders, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Cooper pairing is a fundamental aspect of superconductivity and results in charge transport with a basic unit of 2e (e is the electron charge). This basic unit can manifest itself as the denominator in the formula for Shapiro steps of ac Josephson effect-nhf/(2e), where h is the Planck constant, f is the ac frequency and n is an integer. Violation of this standard formula may suggest unconventional physics, such as nhf/4e for charge-4e superconductivity. Here, we report discovery of a new sequence of nhf/3e in twisted cuprate Josephson junctions. The microwave frequency/power, temperature, and current annealing dependences of this exotic sequence consistently point to hf/3e as the unit for Shapiro steps, indicating a Josephson tunneling process involving a trio of electrons (3e). This bunching of an odd number of charges distinguishes itself from well-established superconducting processes, which are carried by composite bosons made of an even number of charges. The unusual observations highlight the important interplay between competing charge orders in twisted bilayer systems.

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We present a detailed first-principles investigation of the superconducting hydride LiPdH$_2$, focusing on the competition between its tetragonal $P4/mmm$ and rhombohedral $R\bar{3}m$ phases. Our results reveal a pressure-induced phase transition near 220 GPa, where $R\bar{3}m$ overtakes $P4/mmm$ in thermodynamic stability.
