**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22500)

**TL;DR**: Defines a real-space topological index built from the geometry of a quadratic ``landscape'' associated with the zero-energy spatial dynamics of chiral systems. **Tags**: `real-space topology, topological superconductivity, Majorana physics, edge states, quantum geometry, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We define a real-space topological index from the geometry of a quadratic landscape associated with the spatial dynamics, which is governed by the zero-energy eigenvalue equation of chiral systems. The landscape index remains invariant under continuous deformations preserving the uniform exponential separation between decaying and growing spatial solutions and equals the continuum winding number in the homogeneous limit. Across an inhomogeneous interface, the difference between the indices on the two sides equals the protected net chiral zero-mode count, without requiring asymptotically homogeneous bulk regions. Explicit landscape constructions yield analytical bounds on Hamiltonian parameters and their spatial gradients that determine the indices for all profiles satisfying these bounds, without specifying their detailed spatial form. Dimensional reduction extends the landscape index and its boundary correspondence to higher-dimensional systems. The resulting bounds guarantee protected radial zero modes in effective Kitaev-type systems of arbitrary transverse codimension and identify parameter and momentum regions supporting chiral edge states in periodically modulated quantum anomalous Hall systems. For the quasiperiodic interface considered, the index difference guarantees a single protected chiral branch despite persistent modulation on both sides, as confirmed by numerical calculations. Landscape geometry thus connects real-space topology to the protected net chirality of boundary states.
