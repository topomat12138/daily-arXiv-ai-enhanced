<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>

The easy-plane anisotropy supplies the $U(1)$ platform, while the nonrelativistic spin splitting generates sublattice-alternating exchange imbalance with $B_{1g}$ symmetry. Via a continuum theory and Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert simulations, we find that the Goldstone mode becomes quadratic at the critical stability boundary. <details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>

In some systems, the pronounced even-odd conductance oscillation with the walk length can be observed; meanwhile destructive QI can lead to a dramatic suppression of conductance, resulting in a huge on/off ratio. In this paper, we extend the investigation from individual molecules to bipartite lattice systems, providing an abstract framework for exploring QI in a broad range of two-dimensional systems. First, a unit-cell-replicated bipartite subset is constructed by removing a subset of atoms from the input network, and its Green's function map is efficiently obtained from a local recurrence relation. Second, the removed atoms and local structural modifications are coupled back as local degrees of freedom, generating zero-mode vectors through the same Green's function map. Finally, the surviving zero-mode combinations are selected by an effective coupling matrix, whose projectors correct the reference map to yield the final QI pattern. This rule applies to finite flakes with arbitrary boundaries and holes and extends naturally to infinite lattices and vacancy defects.
</details>

  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the crystallographic-orientation dependence of the response of $λ$-(BETS)$_2$GaCl$_4$ ($λ$-Ga) to Bi$_2$Se$_3$ deposition. Cross-sectional focused-ion-beam (FIB) imaging confirms formation of a deposited Bi$_2$Se$_3$-containing layer and a distinct interface with $λ$-Ga. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SC-XRD) shows that the coated $λ$-Ga crystal remains structurally intact but exhibits a strongly orientation-dependent, non-monotonic response, with the largest modification for the $ab$-plane after 4 min of deposition, whereas the $ac$-plane remains comparatively unchanged. This condition corresponds to the intermediate-deposition regime previously identified at a Bi$_2$Se$_3$ thickness of approximately 110 nm. First-principles calculations based on the refined structures show the largest orientation-dependent electronic modification under the same conditions. Complementary optical spectroscopy of Bi$_2$Se$_3$/SiO$_2$ films shows deposition-time-dependent evolution of the film response. These results establish a pronounced crystallographic anisotropy in the structural and calculated electronic response of $λ$-Ga to Bi$_2$Se$_3$ deposition.
