<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>

Kagome superconductors provide a fertile landscape for emergent phenomena arising from strong electronic correlations and nontrivial band topology, yet their superconducting pairing symmetry remains elusive. <details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
In a bulk 3D type-II superconductor, the lower critical field at which an isolated vortex becomes thermodynamically favorable is a size-independent material property. We show that the situation is different in 2D superconductors: the larger the superconductor, the weaker the field needed to create its first vortex. The lower critical field in 2D is always size-dependent. For a disk of area $\mathcal A$, the lower critical field $B_v(\mathcal A)$ scales as $\mathcal A^{-1}\ln(\mathcal A/\mathcal A_0)$ in the weak-screening regime and as $\mathcal A^{-1/2}$ in the strong-screening regime. Our theory shows that every trajectory carrying fixed finite flux ultimately approaches the vortex-free state in the thermodynamic limit and provides a firm microscopic foundation for the weak-field thermodynamics of 2D superconductors.
