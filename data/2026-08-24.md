### [2] [Dissipation driven boundary localization in higher order topological insulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20867)
*Xue Ping Ren, Xiao Ran Wang, Xin Ran Ma, Xi Hu, Su Peng Kou*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20867)

**TL;DR**: In a BHZ-type second-order topological insulator, spatially inhomogeneous, spin-dependent boundary dissipation can relocate both propagating edge modes and corner (Jackiw–Rebbi) states to a common dissipative domain wall without altering the bulk topology. Edge-subspace projection, numerics and a fixed-energy Feshbach reduction identify the dissipative Pauli channel as the mechanism that sets identical transfer exponents and controls the localization envelope.  
**Method**: Analytical projection onto the edge subspace to derive dominant dissipation effects (Pauli-channel analysis), numerical simulations of finite systems to confirm edge-theory predictions, and a fixed-energy Feshbach (non-Hermitian) reduction to capture finite-size effects.  
**Tags**: `higher-order topological insulators, boundary/edge physics, non-Hermitian/dissipation effects, domain-wall localization, Jackiw–Rebbi physics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We study dissipation driven boundary localization in a Bernevig Hughes Zhang type second order topological insulator by introducing inhomogeneous, spin dependent boundary dissipation. After projection onto the edge subspace, the dominant component of the boundary dissipation lies in the same Pauli channel as the kinetic term. This channel gives both counter-propagating edge modes the same real transfer exponent. The two modes therefore accumulate at the same dissipative domain wall. For the corner states, the dissipative envelope competes with Jackiw Rebbi localization. Increasing dissipation moves their weight from the geometric corners to the dissipative domain wall. Propagating edge states also localize at this wall. Numerical calculations confirm the qualitative predictions of the edge theory. A fixed energy Feshbach reduction captures finite size effects. Inhomogeneous boundary dissipation thus controls boundary state localization without changing the bulk topology.
