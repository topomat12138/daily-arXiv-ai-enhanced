### [1] [Anderson localization in topological metals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16124)
*A. A. Burkov*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16124)

**TL;DR**: Disorder can obscure spectral gaplessness, so the authors ask whether the long-range entanglement/topological character of topological metals survives disorder (i.e. resists Anderson localization). Using a topological-response viewpoint (unquantized anomaly) combined with decorated-domain-wall arguments for average SPTs, they find that 3D Weyl semimetals are protected against localization by topology, whereas 2D Dirac, 3D type-I Dirac, and 3D nodal-line semimetals are not.  
**Method**: Combine the concept of an unquantized anomaly as the topological response of gapless topological metals with the decorated domain wall construction previously used to analyze average symmetry-protected topological phases; apply this framework to assess disorder-induced localization and entanglement protection.  
**Tags**: `topological semimetals, Anderson localization, disorder in quantum materials, topological responses, symmetry-protected topological phases`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We address the question of whether topological metals (TM) retain their nontrivial characteristics in the presence of disorder. While in the clean case, the issue is what protects the gaplessness of the spectrum, i.e. the band-touching nodes in TM, this question needs to be reformulated in the presence of disorder. The concept of gaplessness loses its sharp meaning in this case, since spectral gaps can be filled in by disorder-induced states. What is still meaningful is entanglement: one may ask whether the long-range entanglement of the clean gapless TM survives in the presence of disorder or, in other words, whether nontrivial topology protects the TM from Anderson localization. We demonstrate that such a protection exists in the case of three-dimensional (3D) Weyl semimetals, but not in 2D and 3D (type-I) Dirac or 3D nodal line semimetals. We establish this by combining the idea of unquantized anomaly as the topological response of TM with the decorated domain wall construction, used previously to discuss average symmetry-protected topological insulating phases.

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>

Hexagonal warping in topological insulator (TI) surface states generates rich momentum-space geometric textures. Here, we show that proximity-coupling a hexagonally warped TI to a d-wave altermagnet lifts spatial mirror symmetry via the interplay of C_{3v} warping and d-wave spin-splitting.
