  <summary>Abstract</summary>

Here we propose an alternative route: imprint the superlattice optically, using patterned electromagnetic fields rather than a physical twist or strain. ### [2] [On the electronic and vibrational dimensionality of nanometer-scale silicon structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18277)
*Massimo V. Fischetti, Dallin O. Nielsen, Edward Chen*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18277)

**TL;DR**: Paper argues that electronic and vibrational dimensionality in semiconductor nanostructures should be assessed by carrier coherence lengths (inelastic mean free paths). Using literature-based estimates for Si nanosheets at room temperature, they suggest electrons are quantum-confined below ~8 nm (when limited by phonon and remote-phonon losses in gated devices), whereas phonon confinement lacks a single scale: short-wavelength phonons may be confined at ≲10 nm while long-wavelength acoustic phonons can have coherence lengths ∼1 μm.  
**Method**: Conceptual/heuristic analysis: treat electron and phonon inelastic mean free paths (coherence lengths) as the relevant length scales (‘field of view’) and use literature-derived values for Si nanosheets at room temperature to estimate critical dimensions for confinement.  
**Tags**: `quantum confinement, phonon confinement, semiconductor nanostructures, coherence and mean free path, nanophononics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We discuss the problem of assessing the electronic and vibrational dimensionality of a semiconductor nanostructure: How thin and/or wide must a nanostructure be in order to induce electron and phonon confinement? Clarifying the physical justification for common criteria found in the literature, we view the electron coherence length (defined as the electron and phonon inelastic mean free path) as their `field of view' and argue (or, better yet, `speculate') that this sets the important length scale. Considering the example of Si nanosheets at room temperature, and drawing from results found in the literature, we estimate that the critical length below which electrons are subject to quantum confinement is of the order of (or smaller than) 8 nm, when their coherence length is determined by energy losses to phonons and remote phonons in gated structures. On the contrary, no single length-scale can be given for phonons: Taking their coherence length as determined by scattering with electrons and anharmonic three-phonon processes, short wavelength acoustic and optical phonons may be confined only by structures as small as 10 nm. Long-wavelength acoustic phonons, instead, may exhibit a coherence length of the order of 1 micrometer, so that they may be confined over much larger distances.
</details>

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19044)

**TL;DR**: In an attractive Lieb‑lattice model where a noninteracting flat band touches a lower band, interactions generically open a gap that stabilizes bound fermion pairs which Bose‑condense; these pairs form inside the interaction‑induced gap with an anomalously light effective mass, enabling exceptionally high Tc.   
**Method**: Controlled‑precision, nonperturbative numerical protocol applied to the attractive Lieb‑lattice model to resolve bound‑pair formation, interaction‑induced gap formation, and pair effective mass in the band‑touching regime.  
**Tags**: `flat‑band superconductivity, Bose‑Einstein condensation (BEC), unconventional superconductivity, lattice models, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
The formation of bound bosonic pairs of fermions, followed by their Bose-Einstein (quasi) condensation (BEC), is a foundational mechanism of superconductivity. At low filling, flat-band superconductivity is well captured by this mechanism provided the flat band is separated from the occupied lower band by an energy gap. However, particularly high $T_c$ values are anticipated when the non-interacting flat and lower bands touch---as in the prototypical attractive Lieb-lattice model studied here---invalidating the conventional picture: while interactions might protect the bound state by opening a gap, no small parameter guarantees the separation of the bound-state energy from this gap or occupied-band excitations, leaving the pair's fate uncertain. Based on a controlled-precision numerical protocol---which we demonstrate to be essential in this fundamentally non-perturbative problem---we show that the BEC mechanism, underpinned by an interaction-induced gap, is generically robust and remarkably efficient: fermions doped into the flat band form bound pairs within this gap with an anomalously light effective mass, enabling an exceptionally high $T_c$.

### [16] [Ultrasonic attenuation in inhomogeneous superconductors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19079)
*Aman Sardwal, Andreas Kreisel, Shantanu Mukherjee*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19079)

**TL;DR**: Ultrasound attenuation selectively senses subgap bound states via coherence-factor filtering: phase-tunable Andreev bound states in a phase-biased SNS junction give a resonant attenuation when phonon frequency matches level splitting, while large spectral-weight impurity-induced bound states at a (110) d_{x^2-y^2} grain boundary remain weak unless local magnetism produces spin splitting and a clear attenuation peak. The study uses real-space BdG calculations and benchmarks against homogeneous s- and d-wave results.  
**Method**: Real-space Bogoliubov–de Gennes numerical evaluation of ultrasonic attenuation (phonon response) in superconducting geometries, benchmarked against known low-temperature s- and d-wave limits.  
**Tags**: `ultrasonic attenuation, Josephson physics, unconventional superconductivity, impurity physics, magnetism in superconductors`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Subgap bound states can produce pronounced features in the density of states without necessarily giving rise to a corresponding signal in ultrasonic attenuation. We show that the coherence factors act as a filter for the contribution of bound states to ultrasonic attenuation. In a phase-biased superconductor--normal--superconductor junction, the Andreev bound states produce a resonance in the attenuation when the phonon frequency matches the phase-dependent separation between the subgap levels. The situation is different at a $(110)$ grain boundary in a $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductor. There, nonmagnetic impurity-induced bound states remain weak in the attenuation despite their large spectral weight, because the relevant coherence factors nearly cancel. When local magnetism develops near the boundary, this cancellation is removed, the bound states become spin split, and a clear attenuation peak appears, which we argue is governed by a process involving emission of phonons by pair of quasiparticles at low temperatures. We obtain these results using a real-space Bogoliubov--de Gennes formulation of ultrasonic attenuation and benchmark the method against the known low-temperature behavior of homogeneous $s$- and $d$-wave superconductors. The results show that ultrasound can distinguish bound states with similar spectral signatures but different symmetry and spin structure.
</details>


### [17] [Large Field-Free Superconducting Diode Effect with Nonmonotonic Polarity Reversals in NbSe$_2$/CrBr$_3$ Heterostructures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19099)
*Shengbiao Sun, Lang Xiao, Chenghao Shen, Bowen Hao, Jia-Peng Peng, Ziye Zhu, Feiyue Wang, Qilin Han, Ya-Qing Bie, Shuo Wang, Tong Zhou, Dapeng Yu, Ben-Chuan Lin*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19099)

**TL;DR**: Field-free, programmable superconducting diode effect (SDE) with 35.7% efficiency demonstrated in a van der Waals NbSe2/CrBr3 heterostructure. The SDE arises not from finite-momentum pairing but from a leading symmetry-allowed cubic term odd in Cooper-pair momentum; hysteretic, history-dependent diode polarity is attributed to layered ferrimagnetism in CrBr3 producing a reversible interfacial exchange field. Results combine transport, micromagnetic simulation, and generalized Ginzburg–Landau modeling.  
**Method**: Device-level transport measurements on NbSe2/CrBr3 van der Waals heterostructures, supported by micromagnetic simulations of CrBr3 magnetism and a generalized Ginzburg–Landau theoretical analysis incorporating a cubic-in-momentum term in the superconducting free energy.  
**Tags**: `nonreciprocal superconductivity, superconducting diode effect, van der Waals heterostructures, layered magnetism / ferrimagnetism, proximity-induced exchange`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
The superconducting diode effect (SDE), characterized by nonreciprocal dissipationless supercurrent, offers a promising route toward ultralow-power superconducting electronics. Yet most realizations require an external magnetic field, and achieving a large, controllable SDE at zero field remains challenging. Here we report a field-free SDE with an efficiency reaching 35.7% in a van der Waals NbSe$_2$/CrBr$_3$ heterostructure, whose polarity is programmable by magnetic-field history. Unlike conventional mechanisms based on finite-momentum pairing, the SDE originates from the leading symmetry-allowed cubic term odd in Cooper-pair momentum, which yields unequal critical currents while leaving the equilibrium condensate at zero momentum. Remarkably, upon sweeping a perpendicular magnetic field, the diode polarity undergoes nonmonotonic and hysteretic reversals that cannot be explained by Meissner screening or conventional ferromagnetic proximity. Combining transport measurements, micromagnetic simulations, and a generalized Ginzbur-Landau theory, we attribute these unconventional behaviors to layered ferrimagnetism in CrBr$_3$, where coexisting ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic interlayer couplings produce a history-dependent interfacial exchange field acting on NbSe$_2$. Our results reveal a distinct mechanism for nonreciprocal superconductivity and establish layered van der Waals magnetism as a versatile platform for high-efficiency, programmable, field-free superconducting diodes.
</details>
