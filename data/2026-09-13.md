### [1] [Out-of-equilibrium relaxation dynamics of the superconducting order parameter in CsV$_3$Sb$_5$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11686)
*Avi Shragai, Alexander Won, Jonathan M. DeStefano, Andrea Capa Salinas, Ganesh Pokharel, Stephen D. Wilson, B. J. Ramshaw*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11686)

**TL;DR**: Ultrasonic attenuation measurements in the kagome superconductor CsV3Sb5 reveal a peak inside the superconducting state caused by order-parameter relaxation when the relaxation time matches the drive frequency; analysis yields a microscopic scattering time τ_N ≈ 25 ps, implying elastic scattering does not relax the condensate and supporting a sign-preserving s-wave state (Anderson’s theorem).  
**Method**: Frequency-dependent ultrasonic attenuation (acoustic spectroscopy) measured across Tc, analyzed with order-parameter relaxation theory to extract a microscopic scattering time and compared with elastic scattering (resistivity) and inelastic scattering (thermal transport) timescales.  
**Tags**: `superconductivity, order-parameter dynamics, impurity physics, quantum materials, acoustic/ultrasonic spectroscopy`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
The application of a time-varying strain field drives a superconducting order parameter out of equilibrium. How the order parameter relaxes back to equilibrium depends both on the structure of the superconducting gap and on the nature of quasiparticle scattering. We report the discovery of an ultrasonic attenuation peak inside the superconducting state of the kagome superconductor CsV$_3$Sb$_5$. This peak is the natural consequence of the order parameter relaxation time matching the ultrasonic drive frequency near $T_{\rm c}$. From the measured frequency dependence of the peak, we extract a microscopic scattering time of $τ_N = 25$ ps. This timescale is two orders of magnitude longer than the elastic scattering time as determined by resistivity measurements, but is comparable to the inelastic scattering time determined by thermal transport. Within the conventional framework of order-parameter relaxation, this implies that elastic scattering is ineffective at relaxing the superconducting condensate, consistent with a sign-preserving $s$-wave state obeying Anderson's theorem.
</details>

### [2] [Bridging steady-state and time-domain descriptions of molecular electron transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11684)
*Thibaut Lacroix, Namgee Cho, Clemens Vittmann, James Lim, Susana F. Huelga, Martin B. Plenio*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11684)

**TL;DR**: Finite-size time-domain wave-packet transmission equals a spectral average of the energy-resolved NEGF/Landauer transmission weighted by the packet’s energy distribution; by constructing non-Gaussian auxiliary packets that encode the Landauer energy window one can extract I–V curves from time propagation. The correspondence also holds for spin-resolved transport in a spin–phonon model of chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS).  
**Method**: Analytical derivation linking time-dependent wave-packet scattering to steady-state NEGF (Landauer) transmission, construction of tailored non-Gaussian auxiliary wave packets that encode energy windows, and demonstration in a spin–phonon model for spin-resolved (CISS) transport.  
**Tags**: `non-equilibrium Green's functions, time-dependent wave-packet methods, molecular electronics, spintronics, chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS)`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Electron transmission from an input electrode, through a molecular system, to an output electrode has been widely studied using the steady-state non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) method. Recently, the wave packet method, which provides access to the transient dynamics of electrons as well as internal molecular degrees of freedom, has been employed to investigate enantiospecific electron transport through chiral molecules. In this work, we derive the quantitative relation between the transmission of a finite-size wave packet and the energy-resolved NEGF transmission, showing that the former corresponds to a spectral average of the latter weighted by the wave packet energy distribution. Exploiting this correspondence, we construct non-Gaussian auxiliary wave packets whose spectral weight encodes the Landauer energy-window, allowing current-voltage characteristics to be obtained directly from time propagation. We further show that the correspondence extends to spin-resolved transport in a spin-phonon model of chirality-induced spin selectivity.
</details>
