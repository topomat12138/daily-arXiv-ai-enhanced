### [1] [Fermi Surface Geometry from Charge Fluctuations in Three-Dimensional Metals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13951)
*Pok Man Tam, Yarden Sheffer, Xiao-Chuan Wu, F. D. M. Haldane, Shinsei Ryu*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13951)

**TL;DR**: In 3D noninteracting multiband metals the subleading logarithmic term of bipartite charge fluctuations encodes Fermi‑surface shape and quantum geometry: it equals the dimensionless |q|^3 coefficient of the momentum‑space structure factor and both are written as Fermi‑surface integrals of the curvature tensor and the quantum metric. For quadric real‑space partitions (sphere/ellipsoid) this logarithmic coefficient satisfies a topological bound fixed by the Fermi‑surface Euler characteristic and Chern number, demonstrating an interplay between topology and quantum geometry.  
**Method**: Analytical derivation relating real‑space bipartite charge fluctuations to the small‑q expansion of the structure factor (extracting the |q|^3 coefficient), and expressing that coefficient and the logarithmic fluctuation term as surface integrals over the Fermi surface involving the curvature tensor and the quantum metric; derivation of a topological bound for quadric partitions based on Euler characteristic and Chern number.  
**Tags**: `quantum_geometry, topology, Fermi_surface, multi-band_metals, structure_factor, bipartite_fluctuations, Chern_number, Euler_characteristic`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
For three-dimensional non-interacting multi-band metals, we show that important information about the shape and the quantum geometry of Fermi surfaces is encoded in the subleading logarithmic term of bipartite charge fluctuations. This logarithmic term is related to the dimensionless $|\mathbf{q}|^3$-coefficient of the structure factor in momentum space, and both quantities can be expressed as Fermi surface integrals of the Fermi surface curvature tensor and the quantum metric tensor. When the real-space partition surface is a quadric (i.e., sphere or ellipsoid), the logarithmic coefficient satisfies a topological bound depending only on the Euler characteristic and the Chern number of the Fermi surface, illustrating a non-trivial interplay between topology and quantum topology in multi-band metals.
</details>


### [2] [Operator ordering as an emergent geometric background in Dirac systems with spatially varying mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14148)
*C. A. S. Almeida*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14148)

**TL;DR**: Imposing the unique Hermitian ordering of the Dirac Hamiltonian with spatially varying mass (required by probability-current conservation) produces an extra logarithmic-gradient term ∝∇ln m that modifies the effective kinetic operator and yields a universal deformation of the spectral quantization condition. In compact geometry this leads to a mode-dependent second-order spectral shift, strongly enhanced near the mass-inversion threshold, so discrete spectra in inhomogeneous scalar backgrounds acquire observable corrections.  
**Method**: Analytical theoretical derivation: enforce Hermiticity and probability-current conservation for the Dirac operator with spatially varying mass to obtain the additional logarithmic-gradient term; derive the modified effective kinetic operator and universal deformation of the quantization condition; perform explicit analytic calculations in a compact geometry to extract mode-dependent second-order spectral shifts and analyze enhancement near the mass-inversion threshold.  
**Tags**: `topology,Dirac_operator,spectral_shift,mass_inhomogeneity,probability_current_conservation,quantum_geometry`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the spectral consequences of the uniquely determined Hermitian ordering of the Dirac Hamiltonian with spatially varying mass. In contrast to the nonrelativistic case, where continuous families of admissible prescriptions exist, the relativistic Dirac operator admits a single consistent ordering compatible with probability-current conservation. This requirement generates an additional logarithmic-gradient term proportional to the spatial variation of the mass profile. We show that this contribution modifies the effective kinetic operator and induces a universal deformation of the spectral quantization condition. In compact geometry, an explicit analytic computation reveals a mode-dependent second-order spectral shift that becomes strongly enhanced near the mass-inversion threshold. These results demonstrate that the consistent relativistic ordering of the Dirac operator leads to observable modifications of discrete spectra in spatially inhomogeneous scalar backgrounds.
</details>


### [3] [Periodic Behavior of Topology in Graphene with Nanohole Array](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14436)
*Yong-Cheng Jiang, Xing-Xiang Wang, Xiao Hu*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14436)

**TL;DR**: Provides a simple diagnostic rule for when graphene with a triangular or honeycomb nanohole superlattice (m√3×m√3) hosts nontrivial band topology: nontrivial phases recur with period 2 in m for triangular arrays and period 6 for honeycomb arrays, constrained by C6v symmetry.  
**Method**: Symmetry-based topological diagnosis using the superstructure lattice constant m√3×m√3 together with analysis of Wannier-center (Wyckoff) positions and parity eigenvalue indices of valence bands at high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone.  
**Tags**: `topology,quantum_geometry,graphene,superlattice,Wannier_centers,Wyckoff_positions,parity_index,nanoholes,C6v_symmetry`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We derive a way to diagnose band topology for graphene with triangular and/or honeycomb array of nanoholes directly from the lattice constant of superstructure $m\sqrt{3}\times m\sqrt{3}$ with integer $m$. Taking into account the $C_{6v}$ crystalline symmetry respected by nanoholes and their array, we demonstrate that nontrivial topology appears periodically with $m$ with period two (six) for triangular (honeycomb) array. These behaviors are verified by Wyckoff positions of Wannier centers and parity index of valence bands at high-symmetry points in Brillouin zone. The results provide a convenient guide for material design of topological electronic states based on graphene derivatives.
</details>


### [4] [Spin resolved spectral topology and re-entrant localization in a non Hermitian quasiperiodic SSH chain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14639)
*Hemant K Sharma*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14639)

**TL;DR**: Non-Hermitian quasiperiodic SSH chain with Rashba SOC and spin-dependent hopping exhibits a re-entrant extended→localized→extended transition tied to a simultaneous real–complex–real spectral evolution; spin-dependent hopping splits formerly spin-degenerate spectral loops (w=2) into four spin-resolved contours with distinct winding numbers, establishing a direct link between localization, spectral topology, and spin-resolved spectral splitting.  
**Method**: Analysis of inverse participation ratio (IPR), mapping of the complex energy spectrum, and computation of spectral winding numbers for a non-Hermitian quasiperiodic Su–Schrieffer–Heeger model with Rashba spin–orbit coupling and tunable spin-dependent hopping to track localization and topological spectral changes.  
**Tags**: `topology,non-Hermitian,quasiperiodic,SSH_model,Rashba_spin-orbit,spin-dependent_hopping,localization,spectral_winding,re-entrant_transition,spin-resolved_spectra`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate localization and spectral topology in a non Hermitian quasiperiodic Su Schrieffer Heeger lattice with Rashba spin orbit coupling and spin-dependent hopping. By analyzing the inverse participation ratio, complex energy spectrum, and spectral winding numbers, we demonstrate the emergence of a re-entrant transition from extended to localized and back to extended phases as the non-Hermitian parameter increases. The localization transition is accompanied by a simultaneous real-complex-real spectral transition in the complex-energy plane. In the absence of spin dependent hopping, the spectrum forms two nearly spin-degenerate loops characterized by winding numbers w = 2. Upon introducing finite spin-dependent hopping, each loop splits into two independent spin-resolved spectral branches, resulting in four disconnected spectral contours carrying distinct winding sectors. Our results reveal a direct correspondence between localization, spectral topology, and spin-resolved spectral splitting in non-Hermitian quasiperiodic systems.
</details>

### [5] [Dual Shapiro steps and fundamental transconductance in dc driven Bloch transistor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14080)
*A. B. Zorin*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14080)

**TL;DR**: Proposes a superconducting circuit built from a Bloch transistor driven by two dc sources that control Josephson and Bloch oscillations; predicts mutual phase-locking (f_J=f_B) producing current steps I_B=2e f_J and a quantized transconductance 1/R_Q, enabling a magnetic-field-free quantum resistance standard.  
**Method**: Theoretical proposal and dynamical analysis of a Bloch-transistor circuit under two dc drives, studying mutual phase locking between Josephson and Bloch oscillations and deriving consequences for the I–V characteristics (dual Shapiro-like current steps) and transconductance quantization.  
**Tags**: `quantum_devices, superconductivity, Josephson_effect, Bloch_transistor, phase_locking, dual_Shapiro_steps, quantum_standard_of_resistance`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We propose a superconducting circuit based on the Bloch transistor, a quantum device consisting of two small-capacitance Josephson junctions connected in series and having a small island in between. This device is driven by two dc electrical sources controlling Josephson oscillations of frequency $f_J = 2e\overline{V_J}/h$, related to the average voltage $\overline{V_J}$ on the transistor, and Bloch oscillations of frequency $f_B = \overline{I_B}/2e$, related to the average current $\overline{I_B}$ injected into the transistor island. Due to the Bloch transistor properties, these two types of oscillations can mutually phase lock, i.e., $f_J = f_B$. This leads to formation of current steps on the current-voltage curve at $\overline{I}_B = 2ef_J$, which are similar to the dual Shapiro steps appearing at current $\overline{I}=2ef$ under microwave irradiation of frequency $f$. Moreover, transconductance $\overline{I_B}/\overline{V_J}$ takes the fundamental value of $1/R_Q$, where $R_Q = h/4e^2$ is the resistance quantum. The obtained results pave the way to the alternative quantum standard of resistance, based on the superconducting circuit and operating without applying strong magnetic field.
</details>


### [6] [Shear-stress-constrained superconductivity in Ruddlesden-Popper nickelates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14265)
*Liling Sun, Shu Cai, Jinyu Zhao, Qi Wu, Yang Ding, Tao Xiang, Ho-kwang Mao*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14265)

**TL;DR**: Superconductivity in Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates is argued to occur only when local, constrained shear deformation of the Ni–O framework falls within a bounded window; that shear controls octahedral rotations, interlayer Ni–O–Ni bond angle and dz2–dx2−y2 orbital coupling, providing a unified explanation of pressure threshold, reversibility, spatial inhomogeneity, pressure-medium and substrate sensitivity, and reproducibility issues.  
**Method**: Phenomenological structural/mechanistic proposal: link local shear-strain constraints of the metastable RP lattice to octahedral rotations and orbital hybridization (dz2–dx2−y2), using structural and bonding arguments to account qualitatively for diverse experimental sensitivities under pressure and epitaxial constraint.  
**Tags**: `superconductivity,nickelates,strain-engineering,octahedral-rotations,orbital-coupling,pressure-dependence,thin-films,spatial-inhomogeneity`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Ruddlesden-Popper nickelates exhibit superconductivity under pressure in bulk crystals and under epitaxial constraint in thin films, while remaining highly sensitive to sample quality, oxygen content, defects, and stress conditions. We propose that the metastable RP lattice becomes superconducting only when the local constrained deformation of the Ni-O framework falls within a bounded shear-strain window. This deformation controls octahedral rotations, the interlayer Ni-O-Ni bond angle, and coupling between Ni dz2 and dx2-y2 orbitals. This shear-stress-constrained superconductivity scenario unifies the understanding of the pressure threshold, reversibility, spatial inhomogeneity, pressure-medium dependence, film-substrate sensitivity, and reproducibility.
</details>
