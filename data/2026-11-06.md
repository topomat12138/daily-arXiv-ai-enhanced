### [1] [Topological Transitions of Evanescent Waves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07038)
*Ignacio Pavez, Tom Sheppard, Yoonseok Hwang, Hannah M. Price, Frank Schindler*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07038)

**TL;DR**: The paper analyzes decaying/growing (evanescent) eigenmodes inside band gaps of Hermitian tight-binding systems, showing these modes have non-Hermitian dispersions that can host a pair-creating topological transition of in-gap exceptional points protected by spinless time-reversal symmetry. A Z2 spectral index labels energies inside the gap and predicts domain-wall creation of exceptional points; bound-state tails switch from purely exponential to oscillatory when crossing between exceptional points.  
**Method**: Analytical and model-based study using one-dimensional transfer-matrix formalism to obtain complex wavevectors and non-Hermitian band structure of evanescent modes, supported by continuum and tight-binding model calculations and an example anisotropic photonic-crystal realization.  
**Tags**: `non-Hermitian physics, exceptional points, band topology, tight-binding models, photonic crystals`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We study the band structure of evanescent waves, that is, the decaying or growing solutions that live within the gaps of tight-binding Hamiltonians. Although the underlying Hamiltonian is Hermitian, these modes carry complex wavevectors and therefore exhibit a non-Hermitian dispersion. Using the transfer matrix formalism in one dimension, we show that, in addition to the standard exceptional points that appear at the band edges, a different type of exceptional point can arise inside the gap, protected by spinless time-reversal symmetry. Tuning model parameters drives a topological transition where such in-gap exceptional points are nucleated in pairs, mediated by a crossing between purely imaginary momentum branches. We show that this spectral structure is diagnosed by a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ index assigned to every energy inside the gap, with the in-gap exceptional points arising as its domain walls. A generic bound state in the system has a tail that is purely exponential when its energy lies between a band edge and the nearest exceptional point, but becomes oscillatory - with a wavevector differing from the band-edge momentum - when its energy lies between the pair of in-gap exceptional points. We illustrate these results using continuum and tight-binding models, as well as an anisotropic photonic crystal.
</details>
