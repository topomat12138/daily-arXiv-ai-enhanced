### [1] [Periodic Behavior of Topology in Graphene with Nanohole Array](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14436)
*Yong-Cheng Jiang, Xing-Xiang Wang, Xiao Hu*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14436)

**TL;DR**: Paper gives a symmetry-based diagnostic that predicts when graphene with an m√3×m√3 triangular or honeycomb nanohole superlattice hosts nontrivial band topology: topology appears periodically in m (period 2 for triangular arrays, period 6 for honeycomb).  
**Method**: Analytical derivation using C6v crystalline symmetry constraints combined with real-space Wyckoff positions of Wannier centers and momentum-space parity indices of valence bands at high-symmetry points to link supercell integer m to band topology; results are demonstrated/verified via those topological indicators.  
**Tags**: `topology,graphene,superlattice,nanohole_array,Wyckoff_positions,Wannier_centers,parity_index,crystalline_symmetry,C6v,material_design`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We derive a way to diagnose band topology for graphene with triangular and/or honeycomb array of nanoholes directly from the lattice constant of superstructure $m\sqrt{3}\times m\sqrt{3}$ with integer $m$. Taking into account the $C_{6v}$ crystalline symmetry respected by nanoholes and their array, we demonstrate that nontrivial topology appears periodically with $m$ with period two (six) for triangular (honeycomb) array. These behaviors are verified by Wyckoff positions of Wannier centers and parity index of valence bands at high-symmetry points in Brillouin zone. The results provide a convenient guide for material design of topological electronic states based on graphene derivatives.
</details>


### [2] [Spin resolved spectral topology and re-entrant localization in a non Hermitian quasiperiodic SSH chain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14639)
*Hemant K Sharma*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14639)

**TL;DR**: Non-Hermitian quasiperiodic SSH chain with Rashba spin–orbit and spin-dependent hopping exhibits a re-entrant extended→localized→extended transition as the non-Hermiticity increases, accompanied by a simultaneous real–complex–real evolution of the complex spectrum. Without spin-dependent hopping the spectrum forms two nearly spin-degenerate loops (w=2); finite spin-dependent hopping splits each loop into two spin-resolved branches producing four disconnected contours with distinct spectral winding sectors, establishing a direct correspondence between localization, spectral topology, and spin-resolved splitting.  
**Method**: Numerical spectral and localization analysis: compute inverse participation ratio (IPR), plot complex energy spectra, and evaluate spectral winding numbers to track localization transitions and topological sectors as functions of the non-Hermitian parameter and spin-dependent hopping.  
**Tags**: `topology, non-Hermitian, quasiperiodic, localization, Rashba spin-orbit, spectral-winding, spin-resolved`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate localization and spectral topology in a non Hermitian quasiperiodic Su Schrieffer Heeger lattice with Rashba spin orbit coupling and spin-dependent hopping. By analyzing the inverse participation ratio, complex energy spectrum, and spectral winding numbers, we demonstrate the emergence of a re-entrant transition from extended to localized and back to extended phases as the non-Hermitian parameter increases. The localization transition is accompanied by a simultaneous real-complex-real spectral transition in the complex-energy plane. In the absence of spin dependent hopping, the spectrum forms two nearly spin-degenerate loops characterized by winding numbers w = 2. Upon introducing finite spin-dependent hopping, each loop splits into two independent spin-resolved spectral branches, resulting in four disconnected spectral contours carrying distinct winding sectors. Our results reveal a direct correspondence between localization, spectral topology, and spin-resolved spectral splitting in non-Hermitian quasiperiodic systems.
</details>


### [3] [Anisotropic Surface Spin Waves as Signature of A-type Altermagnets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14735)
*Zhoujian Sun, Yiyuan Chen, Tao Yu, Hai-Zhou Lu, X. C. Xie*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14735)

**TL;DR**: Prediction of a novel anisotropic surface spin-wave mode unique to A-type altermagnets: modes show chirality-dependent top/bottom localization and chiral-split constant-frequency contours, providing an experimental signature (stray-field imaging and resonance absorption) to identify A-type altermagnets and enabling spintronic device concepts.  
**Method**: Symmetry-based analysis and theoretical modelling of spin-wave spectra in A-type altermagnets, linking the absence of a translation/inversion relation between spin-opposite sublattices (broken combined spatial-inversion × time-reversal symmetry) to anisotropic, chirality-dependent surface modes; proposed experimental probes via stray-field detection and resonance absorption.  
**Tags**: `altermagnetic, surface_spin_waves, symmetry_breaking, chirality, resonance_absorption, stray_field, spintronics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Altermagnets have attracted intense interest because they have the advantages of both ferromagnets and antiferromagnets. However, their experimental identification remains challenging, in particular for the A-type altermagnets that account for a large group of material candidates. Here, we discover a kind of anisotropic surface spin waves in A-type altermagnets, which is absent in ferromagnets and conventional antiferromagnets. The anisotropic surface spin waves arise directly from the nature of altermagnets, i.e., the spin-opposite sublattices cannot be related by translation or inversion, which breaks the combined spatial-inversion and time-reversal symmetry, leading to the anisotropic surface spin waves with two properties, the chirality-dependent top-bottom positions and chiral split constant frequency contours. We further show that these two properties can be measured experimentally from the stray field and by resonance absorption spectrum, respectively. Our results provide a signature for detecting altermagnets and will inspire spin-based logic and information-storage devices.
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Exciton-polaritons provide a versatile platform for the study of a wide range of phenomena, including polariton lasers, topological polaritons, and bosonic condensation. Transition metal dichalcogenide monolayers host excitons with large oscillator strength and binding energies constituting a robust matter constituent that forms polaritons from cryogenic to room temperature when embedded in optical microcavities. ### [6] [Current induced magneto-optical Kerr effect as a probe of Dirac carriers in Bi$_{1-x}$Sb$_x$ alloy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14975)
*Ryota Miyazaki, Shunzhen Wang, Guanxiong Qu, Yukihiro Marui, Yuta Kobayashi, Masashi Kawaguchi, Masamitsu Hayashi*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14975)

**TL;DR**: Current-induced MOKE in Bi_{1-x}Sb_x is largest in pure Bi and decreases with Sb, with the MOKE amplitude scaling as ρ^{1.7±0.6} and μ_c^{2.0±0.2}. Model calculations attribute this nontrivial scaling to spin currents generated by Dirac electrons (contrasting with parabolic-band free-electron expectations), showing current-induced MOKE can identify the electronic origin of spin currents across materials.  
**Method**: Experimental measurement of current-induced magneto-optical Kerr effect across Bi_{1-x}Sb_x alloys combined with transport characterization (resistivity and mobility), scaling analysis of MOKE vs ρ and μ_c, and model calculations comparing Dirac-electron versus parabolic-band spin-current generation.  
**Tags**: `magneto-optical_Kerr_effect, spin_current, Dirac_electrons, semimetal, transport_scaling, Bi-Sb_alloys, quantum_devices`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We study the current-induced magneto-optical Kerr effect (MOKE) in Bi$_{1-x}$Sb$_x$ semi-metalic alloys. The MOKE signal is found to be the largest in pure Bi ($x=0$), exceeding that of transition metals by nearly four orders of magnitude, and decreases monotonically with increasing Sb concentration. We find the MOKE signal scales with the resistivity ($ρ$) as $ρ^{1.7 \pm 0.6}$ and with the mobility ($μ_\mathrm{c}$) as $μ_\mathrm{c}^{2.0 \pm 0.2}$. Model calculations show that such exponent can be accounted for if the Dirac electrons are responsible for the generation of spin current. This is in contrast to the $ρ^{2}$ and $μ_\mathrm{c}^{-2}$ scaling of the MOKE signal induced by the free electrons in parabolic band. The scaling of the MOKE amplitude with the resistivity also partly accounts for the order of magnitude differences of the signal observed between metals, semimetals, and semiconductors. These results demonstrate that current induced MOKE serves as an effective means to characterize the nature of spin current in materials with diverse electronic structures.
</details>


### [7] [Impurity-induced geometric correlations and fractional quantization in quantum Hall systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15022)
*M. A. Hidalgo*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15022)

**TL;DR**: Proposes a geometric mechanism for fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states in which correlated distributions of ionized impurities within a Landau level coherently couple cyclotron orbits, partially lifting Landau degeneracy to produce fractional energy sublevels; reproduces principal odd‑denominator sequences, predicts strong dependence on impurity geometry and layer separation, and explains the absence of an incompressible plateau at ν=1/2.  
**Method**: Theoretical/modeling proposal: construct an impurity‑induced coherent coupling between cyclotron orbits within a single Landau level that modifies guiding‑center quantization to generate fractional energy sublevels; derive the resulting spectrum, compare to observed odd‑denominator FQH sequences, and analyze stability dependence on impurity geometry and interlayer separation (including a cancellation argument for ν=1/2).  
**Tags**: `quantum_geometry, impurity_physics, fractional_quantum_Hall, Landau_levels, guiding_center_quantization, odd_denominator`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We propose a geometric mechanism for fractional quantum Hall states based on impurity-induced correlations within a Landau level. A correlated distribution of ionized impurities partially modifies the Landau-level degeneracy through coherent coupling between cyclotron orbits, generating fractional energy sublevels. The odd-denominator hierarchy emerges naturally from the intrinsic guiding-center quantization and the correlated cyclotron motion. The resulting spectrum reproduces the principal experimentally observed fractional sequences and predicts a strong dependence of fractional-state stability on impurity geometry and layer separation. The absence of an incompressible Hall plateau at filling factor 1/2 follows from cancellation of the geometric correlations responsible for odd-denominator states. These results suggest that impurity-induced geometry may constitute an additional organizing principle in quantum Hall systems.

  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Focused electron beam induced deposition (FEBID) is an additive manufacturing technique uniquely suited for fabricating nanoscale 3D prototypes for a range of applications, including spintronic devices. ### [9] [Oscillatory photoresistance on the high field side of the cyclotron resonance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15111)
*M. A. Zudov*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15111)

**TL;DR**: Predicts a new class of magnetoresistance oscillations—radiowave-induced resistance oscillations (RIROs)—originating from the displacement contribution to photoresistance in overlapping Landau levels at radiation frequencies much smaller than the cyclotron frequency. In the short-range disorder, high-power limit RIROs have power-independent amplitude, a period set by the radiation electric field (not frequency), and can be 1/B or 1/B^2 periodic with a crossover determined by the cyclotron-resonance absorption width; authors argue these should be experimentally observable.  
**Method**: Analytical/theoretical calculation of the displacement contribution to photoresistance in overlapping Landau levels for ω ≪ ω_c, evaluated in the short-range disorder and high radiation power limit; extraction of oscillation amplitude, period (field-controlled), and B-dependent periodicity with crossover linked to the cyclotron-resonance absorption linewidth.  
**Tags**: `magnetotransport, Landau_levels, radiowave-induced_resistance_oscillations, displacement_mechanism, short-range_disorder, cyclotron_resonance, photoresistance, impurity_physics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We consider the displacement contribution to photoresistance in overlapping Landau levels at radiation frequencies much smaller than the cyclotron frequency. We show that in the limit of short-range disorder and high radiation power, this contribution leads to a new class of magneto-resistance oscillations. These oscillations, which we call radiowave-induced resistance oscillations (RIROs), are distinct from the well known microwave-induced resistance oscillations in the following aspects: (i) their amplitude is independent of power, (ii) their period is controlled by the radiation electric field, rather than by the radiation frequency, and (iii) they can be either $1/B$ or $1/B^2$-periodic, depending on $B$, with the crossover point linked to the width of the cyclotron resonance absorption curve. We also show that RIROs should be readily observed in experiments.
</details>
