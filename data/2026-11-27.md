### [1] [Spin superfluid transport of magnons in altermagnetic insulators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33294)
*Dele Li, Meng-Han Zhang, Dao-Xin Yao*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33294)

**TL;DR**: Altermagnetic Lieb and inverse-Lieb lattices with easy-plane Néel order can support spin superfluid transport. Using continuum theory and LLG simulations the authors show the Goldstone mode softens to a quadratic dispersion at the stability boundary, that phase-gradient-driven spectral transfer depends on flow direction, and that critical spin flow and decay length are anisotropic and controlled by crystal-field splitting, thermal magnons and interfacial spin-mixing conductance. **Tags**: `altermagnetism, spin superfluidity, magnonics, interfacial spin transport, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We study the spin superfluidity for Lieb and inverse Lieb lattices in altermagnets, which enables coherent transport of spin angular momentum carried by the phase of an easy-plane Néel order. The easy-plane anisotropy supplies the $U(1)$ platform, while the nonrelativistic spin splitting generates sublattice-alternating exchange imbalance with $B_{1g}$ symmetry. Via a continuum theory and Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert simulations, we find that the Goldstone mode becomes quadratic at the critical stability boundary. An axial phase gradient transfers phase-channel spectral weight to the gapped mode, whereas a diagonal flow leaves these signatures absent. The spin superfluid exhibits momentum-space anisotropy due to the chiral-splitting magnon dispersion, while strong fields or elevated temperatures suppress phase coherence. Accounting for the contribution of thermal magnons at the interface, we present that the critical spin flow varies with the injection angle under the altermagnetic crystal-field effects. The spin-superfluid decay length scales with the interfacial spin-mixing conductance, which can be measured by the spin Hall magnetoresistance and spin Seebeck effect. Our findings establish altermagnetic insulators, particularly $\mathrm{La_2O_3FeMnSe_2}$, as promising platforms for low-dissipation spin transport.

### [2] [Graphical Rules for Zero-energy Quantum-Interference Patterns in Bipartite Lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33630)

**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33630)

**Method**: Analytical/graphical framework based on Green’s-function maps computed via local recurrence relations on a unit-cell-replicated bipartite subset; treated removed atoms as local degrees of freedom to generate zero-mode vectors; an effective coupling matrix with projectors selects allowed zero-mode combinations to correct a reference conductance map.  
**Tags**: `quantum interference, quantum transport, impurity physics, bipartite lattice models, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Quantum interference(QI) plays an important role in electron transport in molecules, where conductance is determined by the coherent superposition of different quantum-walk paths connecting the source and drain sites. In some systems, the pronounced even-odd conductance oscillation with the walk length can be observed; meanwhile destructive QI can lead to a dramatic suppression of conductance, resulting in a huge on/off ratio. In this paper, we extend the investigation from individual molecules to bipartite lattice systems, providing an abstract framework for exploring QI in a broad range of two-dimensional systems. We propose a simple graphical rule for predicting the QI pattern-the conductance map obtained by varying the drain site while keeping the source site fixed-in bipartite structures with arbitrary boundaries and holes. The analysis can be completed in three steps. First, a unit-cell-replicated bipartite subset is constructed by removing a subset of atoms from the input network, and its Green's function map is efficiently obtained from a local recurrence relation. Second, the removed atoms and local structural modifications are coupled back as local degrees of freedom, generating zero-mode vectors through the same Green's function map. Finally, the surviving zero-mode combinations are selected by an effective coupling matrix, whose projectors correct the reference map to yield the final QI pattern. This rule applies to finite flakes with arbitrary boundaries and holes and extends naturally to infinite lattices and vacancy defects.
</details>

  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the crystallographic-orientation dependence of the response of $λ$-(BETS)$_2$GaCl$_4$ ($λ$-Ga) to Bi$_2$Se$_3$ deposition. Cross-sectional focused-ion-beam (FIB) imaging confirms formation of a deposited Bi$_2$Se$_3$-containing layer and a distinct interface with $λ$-Ga. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SC-XRD) shows that the coated $λ$-Ga crystal remains structurally intact but exhibits a strongly orientation-dependent, non-monotonic response, with the largest modification for the $ab$-plane after 4 min of deposition, whereas the $ac$-plane remains comparatively unchanged. This condition corresponds to the intermediate-deposition regime previously identified at a Bi$_2$Se$_3$ thickness of approximately 110 nm. First-principles calculations based on the refined structures show the largest orientation-dependent electronic modification under the same conditions. Complementary optical spectroscopy of Bi$_2$Se$_3$/SiO$_2$ films shows deposition-time-dependent evolution of the film response. These results establish a pronounced crystallographic anisotropy in the structural and calculated electronic response of $λ$-Ga to Bi$_2$Se$_3$ deposition.
