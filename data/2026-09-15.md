### [1] [Percolation threshold in cluster-assembled nanogranular palladium films and related strategies for hydrogen detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15629)
*A. Debot, D. Balakrishnan, A. M. Philippe, E. Barborini*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15629)

**TL;DR**: Supersonic-cluster-deposited nanogranular Pd films show a percolation window between ≈1.5–2.5 nm; electrical transport measured in situ and after ex situ exposure to 0.25% H2/air exhibits fast (T50%<10 s), partly reversible responses and an irreversible component (tentatively due to H-driven partial coalescence). Implanting Pd clusters in SEBS polymer enhances cluster separation and tunnelling-dominated transport; in those samples hydrogen sensing appears dominated by polymer swelling rather than Pd restructuring. Results point to distinct transduction mechanisms for miniaturized H2 detection.  
**Method**: In-situ electrical transport measurements during Supersonic Cluster Beam Deposition of Pd clusters (to map percolation), followed by ex-situ exposure to 0.25% H2 in air; and implantation of Pd clusters into styrene-ethylene-butylene-styrene (SEBS) polymer to modify cluster separation and probe tunnelling vs structural-change sensing.  
**Tags**: `nanostructured films, percolation, quantum tunnelling, hydrogen sensing, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Electrical transport in cluster-assembled nanogranular palladium films was investigated in-situ during growth by Supersonic Cluster Beam Deposition. The percolation onset is observed at a thickness of 1.5 nm, while the percolation completion at 2.5 nm. Films at the percolation thickness (2 nm) and beyond the percolation end (5 nm) were subsequently exposed ex-situ to 0.25% hydrogen in air to investigate how hydrogen-induced structural changes in Pd clusters modulate electrical transport and can be exploited as mechanism for selective hydrogen detection. Implantation of palladium clusters in soft polymer styrene-ethylene-butylene-styrene was also explored to promote clusters separation and enhance quantum tunnelling phenomena within electrical transport. The observed electrical transport pattern features stable baselines in air, suggesting material stability, fast response time (T50% < 10 s), and the coexistence of reversible and irreversible components in conduction changes induced by hydrogen exposure. The irreversible component is tentatively attributed to hydrogen-promoted partial coalescence of Pd clusters. The behaviour of the polymer implanted samples suggests an alternative gas detection mechanism where hydrogen-induced polymer swelling dominates over palladium structural modifications. These results disclose novel possible transducing routes for miniaturized hydrogen detection and multimodal sensing.
</details>

### [2] [Topological superconductivity in an altermagnet-superconductor heterostructure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13408)
*Michael Liudeng, Hrishikesh Patel, Marcel Franz, Niclas Heinsdorf*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13408)

**TL;DR**: Proposes an experimentally realistic heterostructure: an altermagnetic monolayer V2Se2O proximitized by a conventional s-wave superconductor. Momentum-dependent exchange splitting of the altermagnet promotes equal-spin pairing and converts the induced order into a fully gapped p-wave state with nonzero Chern number and chiral Majorana edge modes; the work also quantifies inverse-proximity effects across finite multilayer stacks.  
**Method**: Start from ab-initio electronic structure of V2Se2O, derive a low-energy model of its spin-split Fermi surface, couple it to an s-wave superconductor, solve Bogoliubov–de Gennes equations for the heterostructure, compute Chern numbers and slab spectra, and analyze inverse-proximity/gap evolution in finite multilayers.  
**Tags**: `topological superconductivity, Majorana physics, altermagnetism, triplet superconductivity, proximity effect, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Fully gapped spin-triplet superconductors offer a natural setting for topological edge transport and Majorana excitations. Here we propose a heterostructure that interfaces altermagnetic monolayer V$_2$Se$_2$O with a conventional $s$-wave superconductor. Starting from an ab-initio description of V$_2$Se$_2$O, we construct a low-energy model of its spin-split Fermi surface and study the superconducting instability of the coupled stack. We find that the momentum-dependent exchange splitting of the altermagnet strongly favors equal-spin pairing and converts the proximity-induced order into a fully gapped $p$-wave state. Over a broad range of interface parameters, the resulting Bogoliubov-de Gennes bands carry a nonzero Chern number, and slab spectra exhibit chiral Majorana edge modes traversing the bulk gap. We further quantify the inverse proximity effect and show how the induced gap evolves across a finite multilayer stack. These results establish a route to topological triplet superconductivity by interfacing a conventional superconductor with an experimentally available altermagnetic semiconductor, and provide practical guidance for interface fabrication and experimental detection.
</details>


### [3] [Spatial instability of a homogeneous superconductor after an interaction quench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13426)
*Medha Roy Choudhury, Sankha Subhra Bakshi, Pinaki Majumdar, Amit Ghosal*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13426)

**TL;DR**: Study of an attractive-Hubbard-model superconductor driven far from equilibrium by a sudden quench of the interaction: time-evolution of the full density matrix via Heisenberg equations shows initially homogeneous temporal oscillations of pairing and density that can become unstable after a quench-strength- and initial-perturbation-dependent threshold time, producing transient spatial textures and bond-current inhomogeneities before relaxing toward reduced heterogeneity. Two broad classes of global-order-parameter response are identified, one featuring strongly inhomogeneous pairing and bond currents.  
**Method**: Time evolution of the full many-body density matrix using the Heisenberg equation of motion for the attractive Hubbard model; tracking spatiotemporal dynamics of pairing and density fields starting from weakly inhomogeneous initial conditions following a sudden quench of the Hubbard attractive interaction.  
**Tags**: `nonequilibrium superconductivity, quantum quenches, attractive Hubbard model, spatiotemporal dynamics, superconducting instabilities`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the quantum dynamics of a conventional superconductor, described by the attrac- tive Hubbard model, driven far from equilibrium by quenching the Hubbard attraction. We evolve the full density matrix of the system in time using the Heisenberg equation of motion, tracking the spatiotemporal dynamics of the pairing and density fields. We use very weakly inhomogeneous initial conditions to probe the stability of the homogeneous dynamics to spatial perturbations. At initial times after the quench the pairing and density fields remain homogeneous in space while fluctuating in time. However, depending on the quench strength the spatially uniform dynamics can become unstable beyond a threshold time, with emergence of spatial textures. The onset time for the instability depends on the strength of the quench and the initial inhomogeneity. At long times the system evolves towards a state with significantly reduced spatial heterogeneity. The time dependence of the global superconducting order parameter suggests two broad categories of re- sponse to a quench. One of these involves strongly inhomogeneous pairing and bond currents in the nonequilibrium superconductor.
</details>
