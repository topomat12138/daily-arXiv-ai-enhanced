  <summary>Abstract</summary>

While such a practice has been widely applied to detect the Abelian band topology or the Euler-class topology of band nodes, little is known regarding the quench dynamics of non-Abelian topological insulators in multiband systems, where the intertwining geometry of all eigenstates necessitates the description of noncommutative topological charges. ### [3] [Anisotropy lifts topological protection of transport in pumped spin chains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08385)
*Mainak Pal*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08385)

**TL;DR**: Momentum-space Chern numbers in a dimerized spin-1/2 anisotropic XY chain predict Thouless-type spin pumping, but real-space time evolution with open boundaries shows spin transport is not quantized except in the isotropic limit; anisotropy causes hybridization of transporting states and protocol-dependent, nonconserved spin transport, so momentum-space invariants alone do not guarantee quantized pumping.  
**Method**: Map spin chain to fermions via Jordan–Wigner, analyze momentum-space band topology (Chern numbers) of the resulting extended Rice–Mele model, and perform real-space time-evolution under open boundary conditions to compute driven spin transport and compare isotropic vs anisotropic regimes.  
**Tags**: `topological transport, Thouless pumping, spin chains, real-space dynamics, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We investigate the prospect of topological spin pumping and transport in a dimerized spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ anisotropic XY chain with tunable in-plane anisotropy. Through a Jordan-Wigner transformation, the system maps onto an extended Rice-Mele-type fermionic model whose momentum-space band structure exhibits gapped regions characterized by nonzero Chern numbers ($\pm 1$) as well as trivial and gapless regimes. Conventional momentum-space analysis therefore predicts quantized Thouless-type spin pumping in parts of the Chern number diagram. However, real-space time evolution under open boundary conditions reveals that the spin transport is generally not quantized away from the isotropic limit. In the anisotropic regime, eigen-energy states within the transporting subspace hybridize during the driving cycle, leading to a transport that depends on details of the driving protocol. Quantized pumping and transport survive only in the isotropic limit, where the Hamiltonian reduces to decoupled Rice-Mele sectors and the transported spin component is conserved. Our results demonstrate that momentum-space topological invariants alone do not guarantee quantized spin transport in anisotropic spin chains. More broadly, they show that the conventional Thouless-pumping framework does not straightforwardly extend to situations where the transported quantity is not conserved, highlighting the importance of real-space diagnostics in topological transport phenomena.
</details>
