### [1] [Pairing-channel reconstruction by Ising spin-orbit coupling in a multiorbital triangular-lattice model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21369)
*Lichuan Wang, Yuhang Xiao, Ran Wang, Xinliang Huang, Ning Hao*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21369)

**TL;DR**: In a noncentrosymmetric two-orbital triangular-lattice model with Rashba, Ising, and onsite spin–orbit couplings, the authors show (via multiorbital RPA) that Ising SOC qualitatively reshapes low-energy Fermiology and pairing-phase space, changing the hierarchy of spin-fluctuation–mediated channels. Orbital hybridization lowers the Ising-SOC strength needed to stabilize an E-representation state with dominant S_z=0 triplet character and generates strong competition with an A_1 sign-changing s± singlet. The A_1 state can be a fully gapped, time-reversal-invariant topological superconductor with a nonzero Z_2 index, Wilson-loop spectral flow, and boundary modes arising cooperatively from intra- and inter-orbital pairing.  
**Method**: Construct a noncentrosymmetric two-orbital triangular-lattice Hamiltonian including Rashba, Ising, and onsite SOC; solve the interaction-driven pairing problem with a multiorbital random-phase-approximation (RPA) treatment of spin-fluctuation–mediated superconductivity; classify pairing channels (E and A_1), characterize spin structure (S_z components), and diagnose topology via Z_2 index and Wilson-loop / boundary-mode analysis.  
**Tags**: `unconventional superconductivity, topological superconductivity, noncentrosymmetric superconductivity, multiorbital effects, spin–orbit coupling`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Ising spin-orbit coupling (SOC) is widely known for locking electronic spins and protecting superconductivity against in-plane paramagnetic pair breaking. Here we show that, in a correlated multiorbital system, Ising SOC can play a qualitatively different role: it reconstructs the hierarchy of spin-fluctuation-mediated pairing channels by reshaping the low-energy Fermi surfaces, orbital-spin coherence factors, and momentum-resolved scattering phase space. We study a noncentrosymmetric two-orbital triangular-lattice model with Rashba SOC, Ising SOC, and onsite SOC. We solve the pairing problem within a multiorbital random-phase-approximation framework. In the single-orbital limit, an $E$-representation state with a dominant $S_z=0$ component emerges only at relatively strong Ising SOC. Orbital degrees of freedom facilitate the stabilization of this pairing state at a smaller nominal Ising SOC strength and generate a broad competition between $E$ and $A_1$ pairing channels. The $E$ state can maintain the same $S_z=0$ triplet character, whereas the $A_1$ state becomes a fully gapped sign-changing $\splusminus$ superconductor. The latter also realizes a topologically nontrivial phase in the time-reversal-invariant weak-pairing regime, with a nonzero $\mathbb{Z}_2$ index, Wilson-loop spectral flow, and boundary modes. Its topology is not carried by a single orbital pairing component, but emerges cooperatively from the dominant intra- and inter-orbital pairing components. These results establish a microscopic route by which Ising SOC and orbital hybridization can reconstruct interaction-driven superconductivity, producing both Ising-triplet and topological singlet-dominated phases in correlated non-centrosymmetric systems.
</details>


### [2] [Unique Charge-density-wave Order and Fluctuation-induced Phonon Softening in the Optimally-doped Trilayer Cuprate Bi$_2$Sr$_2$Ca$_2$Cu$_3$O$_{10+δ}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21305)
*S. Nakata, K. Yamamoto, J. Okamoto, D. Shiga, R. Takahashi, H. Y. Huang, H. Kumigashira, H. Wadati, A. Fujimori, S. Ishida, H. Eisaki, D. J. Huang, J. Miyawaki, H. Suzuki*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21305)

**TL;DR**: RIXS measurements on optimally-doped trilayer Bi2223 detect a coherent in-plane CDW at q_CDW=(0.25,0) across the three CuO2 layers, resolve CDW fluctuations whose Cu L3 and O K-edge profiles differ, and show phonon softening where these fluctuations couple to the phonon branch — indicating the RIXS phonon anomaly is driven by CDW fluctuation–phonon coupling.  
**Method**: Cu L3- and O K-edge resonant inelastic x-ray scattering (RIXS) measurements on optimally-doped Bi2Sr2Ca2Cu3O10+δ (Bi2223).  
**Tags**: `charge-density waves, cuprate superconductors, quantum materials, electron-phonon coupling, multilayer (trilayer) superconductors`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Charge-density-wave (CDW) order is a ubiquitous feature of cuprate superconductors that competes with superconductivity. However, its behavior remains poorly understood in trilayer compounds, which contain the optimal number of CuO$_2$ layers for superconductivity. Here, we report Cu $L_3$- and O $K$-edge resonant inelastic x-ray scattering (RIXS) measurements of optimally-doped Bi$_{2}$Sr$_{2}$Ca$_{2}$Cu$_{3}$O$_{10+δ}$ (Bi2223), the trilayer cuprate with the highest superconducting transition temperature ($T_c$) among Bi-based cuprates. We observe CDW order with a single in-plane wavevector of ${\bm q}_{\text{CDW}} = (0.25, 0)$, demonstrating that a coherent CDW order is formed across the trilayers. We also resolve CDW fluctuations emanating from the static order, whose profiles differ between the Cu $L_3$ and O $K$ edges. The phonon softening occurs where these CDW fluctuations interact with the phonon branch, indicating that the RIXS phonon anomaly is driven by the coupling between CDW fluctuations and the phonon branch.
</details>


### [3] [$d$-wave superconductivity in quintuple-layer bulk superconductor La$_6$Ni$_5$O$_{12}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22212)
*Zhan Wang, Yuxin Wang, Hui-Ke Jin, Kun Jiang, Jiangping Hu*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22212)

**TL;DR**: Variational Monte Carlo on an effective five-layer Hubbard model for quintuple-layer La6Ni5O12 finds a spin-singlet d_{x^2-y^2} superconducting ground state (extended-s suppressed). Calculated observables — V-shaped tunneling DOS, linear low-T superfluid density, Knight-shift drop, and 1/T1T∝T^2 — consistently indicate a clean nodal d-wave superconductor. Applying the same framework to related reduced Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates yields a robust d-wave tendency in LaNiO2 and a near-critical trilayer La4Ni3O8.  
**Method**: Construct an effective multi-layer Hubbard model (five-layer for La6Ni5O12) and optimize superconducting variational wavefunctions using variational Monte Carlo; compute experimental observables (tunneling DOS, superfluid density, Knight shift, spin-lattice relaxation) from the optimized state and extend the same modeling to other reduced RP nickelates.  
**Tags**: `unconventional superconductivity, pairing symmetry, quantum materials, strongly correlated electrons, nickelate superconductors`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
The recent realization of bulk superconductivity in the quintuple-layer nickelate La$_6$Ni$_5$O$_{12}$ opens new opportunities to probe the pairing symmetry of the reduced Ruddlesden--Popper (RP) nickelate family. Using an effective five-layer Hubbard model and variational Monte Carlo calculations, we find that a spin-singlet $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave state is energetically favored, while extended-$s$-wave pairing is strongly suppressed. Based on the optimized superconducting state, we further calculate several experimentally accessible observables. The tunneling density of states exhibits a characteristic V-shaped low-energy spectrum, the superfluid density shows a linear low-temperature depletion, the Knight shift decreases below $T_c$, and the spin-lattice relaxation rate follows $1/T_1T\propto T^2$, together providing consistent signatures of a clean nodal superconductor. Extending the same framework to related reduced RP nickelates, we find a robust $d$-wave tendency in infinite-layer LaNiO$_{2}$, while trilayer La$_4$Ni$_3$O$_{8}$ at its nominal filling lies close to the boundary between $d$-wave superconductivity and a non-superconducting state. These results establish bulk La$_6$Ni$_5$O$_{12}$ as a promising platform for determining the pairing symmetry of nickelate superconductors and suggest a common $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave pairing tendency across the reduced RP nickelate family.
</details>


### [4] [Band-Resolved Superconducting Gaps via the Thermal Hall Effect in CeRu2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22361)
*Darian Hall, Luke Pritchard Cairns, Nico Rowland, Yuanqi Lyu, Kohtaro Yamakawa, Josue Rodriguez, Kirin Chanteloup, Didier Perrodin, Joel E. Moore, James G. Analytis*

**Category**: cond-mat.supr-con  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22361)

**TL;DR**: Thermal Hall transport, constrained by specific heat and normal-state magnetotransport, uniquely assigns the larger superconducting gap (Δ_e/k_B ≈ 13 K) to the heavy Ce 4f electron Fermi sheets and the smaller gap (Δ_h/k_B ≈ 8 K) to the hole sheets in compensated heavy-fermion CeRu2; a low-field sign reversal of κ_xy provides the key experimental signature.  
**Method**: Combined analysis of thermal Hall conductivity (κ_xy) and longitudinal thermal conductivity (κ_xx), constrained by specific-heat data and normal-state magnetotransport to fix band mobilities and gap magnitudes in a multiband modeling of CeRu2’s superconducting state.  
**Tags**: `multiband superconductivity, heavy-fermion superconductors, thermal Hall transport, transport-based gap assignment`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Standard bulk probes of multiband superconductors cannot assign pairing gaps to specific electron or hole Fermi surfaces. Here we show that thermal Hall transport ($κ_{xy}$), constrained by specific heat and normal-state magnetotransport, resolves this ambiguity in the compensated heavy-fermion superconductor CeRu$_2$. Fixing the gap magnitudes ($Δ_e/k_B\approx 13$K, $Δ_h/k_B\approx 8$K) and band mobilities completely determines the electronic degrees of freedom in $κ_{xx}$ and $κ_{xy}$. A low-field sign reversal in $κ_{xy}$ places the larger gap on the heavy Ce $4f$ electron sheets and the smaller on the hole sheets.
</details>
