<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We propose an altermagnetism-induced route to a two-dimensional higher-order topological Dirac semimetal in a trilayer nonsymmorphic Dirac system. Interlayer hybridization preserves the nonsymmorphic bulk Dirac crossings and generates helical edge states in the trilayer phase. Introducing outer-layer $d$-wave altermagnetic order gaps the helical edge states, while only the symmetry-protected Dirac crossing at high-symmetry point $M$ remains gapless. ### [2] [Chirality rules for optical vortices in rhombohedral graphene multilayers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04076)
*Felipe Pérez Riffo, Matías Castro Schnaidt, Juan M. Florez, Eric Suárez Morell*

**Category**: cond-mat.mes-hall  
**Abstract**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04076)

**TL;DR**: Rhombohedral (ABC) graphene multilayers host protected optical zeros—momenta where the interband dipole vanishes—whose dipole-phase vortices carry integer charge fixed by the stacking chirality N. The paper shows the total vortex charge near the K valley equals N−1 per linear polarization, with the K point an exact optical zero for N≥3 (except Bernal bilayer), satellites created by trigonal warping whose radii scale with warping, and robustness to interlayer bias and remote hoppings; the band-edge shift current scales linearly with N.  
**Method**: Analytical/topological characterization of interband dipole-phase vortices combined with valley-resolved counting arguments and verification of robustness using the full tight-binding model including trigonal warping and remote hoppings; prediction of consequences for the quantized interband character and shift-current magnitude.  
**Tags**: `quantum geometry, optical response, multilayer graphene, valley physics, vortex physics, nonlinear optics`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
Optical zeros are momenta where the interband dipole vanishes. The dipole phase winds around each of them by an integer, so they are vortices of the resonant optical response with a conserved charge. We show that in rhombohedral graphene multilayers one integer, the stacking chirality $N$, fixes their constellation near the $K$ valley: the total charge per linear polarization is $N-1$ and is conserved under trigonal warping and interlayer bias; for $N\ge3$ the $K$ point stays an exact optical zero, the Bernal bilayer being the only exception; warping places the remaining charge on satellites whose radii scale linearly with the warping; and the charge pinned at $K$ is bias independent for $N\ge4$. A quantized interband character counts the enclosed charge as an integer staircase with steps at these radii, and an interlayer bias drives a satellite through the protected $K$ vortex in the trilayer. The rules survive the remote hoppings of the full tight-binding model and, being integer charges, the corrections that reshape the bands. The same integer sets the magnitude of the response: the band-edge shift current grows linearly with $N$.
</details>


### [3] [Field-free transverse response in altermagnet and topological insulator heterojunctions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04937)

**Tags**: `altermagnetism, topological insulators, transverse/anisotropic transport, spintronics, quantum materials`

<details>
  <summary>Abstract</summary>
We theoretically investigate the transport properties of a topological insulator (TI) connected to two-dimensional altermagnetic (AM) leads in the absence of an external magnetic field. Exploiting the intrinsic time-reversal symmetry breaking of altermagnets, we report the emergence of a field-free transverse differential conductivity in the resulting AM-TI-AM heterostructure. Our analysis reveals that this transverse response is highly sensitive to the orientation of the Néel vector: a finite transverse conductivity manifests when the Néel vector possesses an out-of-plane component, whereas it strictly vanishes for in-plane orientations ($θ= π/2$) under symmetric chemical potentials ($μ_t = μ_b$) due to the restoration of symmetry between the $k_y$ and $-k_y$ momentum channels. Notably, the oscillatory behavior of the longitudinal conductivity persists even at finite temperatures. Spatially, the transverse current contribution decays exponentially with position inside the TI region. We also find that both the longitudinal and transverse differential conductivity exhibit periodic oscillations with the junction parameter $χ_t=χ_b$. These findings provide a fundamental understanding of field-free transverse responses in spin-momentum locked boundary states, highlighting the potential of AM-TI heterojunctions for next-generation spintronic applications.
</details>
